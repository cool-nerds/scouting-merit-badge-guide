Step 01 / 12

Confirm eligibility and the local process

You must be at least 18, have suitable character and subject knowledge, and receive council approval for each badge. Ask your troop’s advancement coordinator and Spirit of Adventure Council ↗ for the current counselor registration route, fees, signatures and submission contact. Existing troop registration alone does not approve you as a counselor.

Step 02 / 12

Choose your merit badges

Review Official merit badge requirements ↗. Choose subjects you know well and list your relevant experience, education or certifications. Check badge-specific counselor and activity-supervisor qualifications in Guide to Advancement ↗ and Guide to Safe Scouting ↗.

Step 03 / 12

Create or recover your Scouting account

Open My.Scouting ↗. Use your existing account and member ID if you already have one; ask council to resolve duplicate records. An account is a login, not counselor approval.

Step 04 / 12

Complete Safeguarding Youth training

Follow Safeguarding Youth training ↗ and complete the required training through My.Scouting ↗. Save the completion certificate and verify that your member record shows current training. Confirm current renewal rules in the official resources.

Step 05 / 12

Learn the counselor role

Find Merit Badge Counselor Position-Specific Training in My.Scouting ↗. Read the Guide for Merit Badge Counseling ↗. Review the EDGE teaching tips and advancement standards below. Ask council what training it requires for approval or renewal.

Step 06 / 12

Prepare adult registration and disclosures

Follow council’s current online or paper registration process for merit badge counselor, position code 42. The national adult application ↗ is a reference if paper forms are requested. Complete required background-check authorizations and disclosures. Confirm fees with council rather than relying on older forms.

Step 07 / 12

Complete Massachusetts CORI requirements

Ask Spirit of Adventure Council ↗ for its current CORI form, identity-verification instructions and secure submission method. Review Massachusetts CORI information ↗. A national background-check authorization does not by itself establish that council’s CORI process is complete. Do not obtain a substitute form without council guidance.

Step 08 / 12

Complete the counselor information form

The council publishes a Official council counselor form (current membership page link) ↗. Confirm it is still accepted and obtain any required unit approval. List each requested badge and qualifications. The national counselor information form ↗ is also available; use the version council directs. Older forms may contain outdated training or fee language.

Step 09 / 12

Submit the complete application

Use the council-confirmed recipient and secure method. Include the required registration materials, counselor form, training certificate, CORI documentation and qualifications as directed. Retain copies securely. This guide does not send or process applications.

Step 10 / 12

Receive approval for your listed badges

Wait for council confirmation of registration and the specific badges approved. Follow up through Spirit of Adventure Council ↗ if needed. A submitted form, checked box or training certificate is not approval. Record only the badges council actually approved.

Step 11 / 12

Verify Scoutbook and arrange a safe first session

Use Scoutbook ↗ and the Scoutbook Plus counselor guide ↗. Ask the unit to connect or assign you as appropriate; contact council if your position or approved badges are missing. Before meeting, review the youth-protection rules below and arrange the required supervision.

Step 12 / 12

Counsel, document and renew

Counsel only your approved badges while registration and training are current. Agree on the applicable requirements with each Scout, assess each Scout individually, and record completed requirements or partials in the unit’s accepted system. Track renewal and certification deadlines with Spirit of Adventure Council ↗.

My planning notes

Keep these notes general. Complete official forms separately.

Teach with EDGE. Approve the exact requirements.

EDGE is a useful teaching approach. It does not replace the wording of a merit badge requirement or add a new requirement.

Explain

Describe the skill, its purpose and the relevant safety steps. Invite questions and connect it to the Scout’s experience.

Demonstrate

Model the skill clearly, with an explanation of what you are doing. Your demonstration prepares the Scout to practice; it does not fulfill a requirement that asks the Scout to demonstrate.

Guide

Let the Scout practice. Offer feedback, encouragement and another chance where needed. Use appropriate qualified supervision for activities that require it.

Enable

Give the Scout room to apply the skill with growing independence, while maintaining required safety and supervision. Confirm that the Scout personally fulfills the actual requirement.

Keep advancement fair and consistent

Demonstrate means demonstrate. Discuss means discuss. Explain means explain. If a requirement asks for a demonstration, a worksheet or watching another person is not a substitute. If it asks for discussion, each Scout must participate. Assess every Scout individually in group instruction.

Do not add, remove or rewrite requirements. Do not add attendance, essays, tests or performance standards as conditions for earning a badge unless the applicable requirement calls for them. Work from the official requirements and current transition instructions; use the authorized advancement process for any permitted special-needs alternatives. Sign off only requirements personally fulfilled, and preserve partial completion records.

Use Official merit badge requirements ↗ and Guide to Advancement ↗, especially section 7 on merit badges. Consult the current guidance when requirements change or a completion question arises.

Youth protection at every session

One-on-one adult–youth contact is prohibited in person and through electronic communication. Include a parent/guardian or another registered adult in electronic communications. Plan supervision using the current rules; do not assume that another youth alone satisfies adult-supervision requirements.

Scouting activities generally require two registered adult leaders age 21 or older, with additional requirements when female youth participate. The official FAQs address a specific merit badge session exception when a Scout’s parent or legal guardian attends the entire session. Review that exception with unit leadership before relying on it, and follow all other applicable safety rules.

Read Youth Protection and Adult Leadership ↗, Youth Protection FAQs ↗ and Guide to Safe Scouting ↗. Counselor registration by itself does not qualify an adult for every other volunteer role or overnight participation.

Parent meeting readout

“Our Scouts benefit from adults who can share skills, interests and experience. You can help by becoming an approved merit badge counselor. Start with this guide, choose subjects you know, complete current Safeguarding Youth training, and follow Spirit of Adventure Council’s registration and screening instructions. Council must approve both your counselor registration and the specific badges before you begin counseling.

“Counselors teach and mentor; Scouts do the work. We follow the exact requirements. Demonstrate means demonstrate, discuss means discuss, and explain means explain. We neither add requirements nor skip them, and every Scout must personally complete the work, including in a group class.

“We use Explain, Demonstrate, Guide and Enable to help Scouts learn, while following current youth-protection and activity-safety rules. There is no one-on-one contact, including online. Work with troop leadership to plan appropriate supervision, record completed requirements and keep your registration, training and any special certifications current.

“Your checklist is private to your browser. It does not register you or send forms. Please confirm current submission instructions with council, use its approved method for sensitive paperwork, and keep a copy for your records. Thank you for helping Scouts discover what they can do.”
